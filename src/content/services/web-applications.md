A web application is a real system doing a real job — not a brochure page. If your practice, shop, or team is running a handful of spreadsheets and WhatsApp messages, this is usually the step that fixes it.

## What this means in practice

A complete application your people use every day to get work done:

- **Register records** — patients, customers, orders, cases, inventory
- **Manage the workflow** — appointments, queues, stock levels, follow-ups
- **See what's happening** — dashboards and reports that owners can read at a glance
- **Control access** — each user sees exactly what their role allows

The stack is the battle-tested MERN family — React on the front, Node.js and Express for the API, MongoDB (or Postgres via Supabase) for data. It runs in any modern browser, on any device, from a phone to an old desktop.

## Where it fits best

Built-for-purpose systems like:

- **Clinics & small hospitals** — patients, appointments, staff, billing
- **Retail & service businesses** — POS, inventory, orders
- **Field teams** — job tracking, attendance, reports from the ground
- **Any operation** drowning in record-keeping done by hand

## Why I build them offline-first

Most app developers assume the internet is always there. In a busy clinic in the middle of a power cut, it isn't. That's why the system writes to the local device first and syncs when the connection returns — the queue keeps moving even when everything else has stopped.
